## TL;DR
Ross Taylor (former reasoning lead at Meta AI) and Chengxi Taylor (co-founder and president of General Reasoning) trace the evolution of language model reasoning from the Galactica era through modern RL-based training, arguing that long-horizon tasks represent the next major frontier. They cover the algorithmic, environmental, and computational challenges of scaling agents to tasks that span weeks or more, introducing concepts like compaction, value models, pipeline RL, and their Kelly benchmark, where all frontier models lost money trading Premier League football matches.

## Key Takeaways
- **RLHF was the decisive factor that made LLMs products.** Galactica had a state-of-the-art base model but failed publicly because it lacked RLHF, while ChatGPT succeeded—constituting a "natural experiment" proving RL's value.
- **A good base model is necessary but not enough.** Galactica outperformed much larger models (PaLM 540B, Chinchilla, GPT-3.5) on scientific domains with far less compute, yet still failed without proper post-training.
- **Galactica pioneered several now-standard ideas:** high-quality curated data over raw token quantity, multi-epoch training, and "thinking tokens" (internal reasoning within tags before producing an answer).
- **Ross's team at Meta applied RL to reasoning tags before DeepSeek R1**, achieving SOTA math results, but lacked the emergent reflective/backtracking behavior because base models, context windows, and RL compute were insufficient—confirming the "bitter lesson."
- **Long-horizon tasks require a mindset shift**, not just engineering. Solving humanity's biggest problems (curing cancer, Mars exploration) demands patience and thinking in terms of years, not single interactions.
- **Context windows are fundamentally scarce for long horizons.** Fermat's Last Theorem might require tens or hundreds of billions of tokens; current windows are only ~1 million, necessitating compaction strategies.
- **Three core RL challenges arise with long horizons:** gradient variance scaling with length, sparse rewards with credit assignment problems, and variable trajectory lengths.
- **Value models (critics) address several long-horizon issues** by reducing variance, fitting compaction, encouraging batch diversity, and enabling bootstrapping—though they add complexity by requiring a separate model.
- **All frontier models failed the Kelly benchmark**, losing money trading Premier League football over a one-year horizon, revealing that current AI is too biased toward coding/procedural tasks and insufficiently handles open-ended, real-world complexity.
- **Pipeline RL trades off-policy staleness for GPU utilization**, but multi-week inference eventually exceeds acceptable off-policy limits (around 8 steps), forcing trade-offs between idle GPUs and value model bias.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Introduction and Talk Structure [00:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=12s)
Ross Taylor introduces himself as CEO of GR (General Reasoning), a London-based reinforcement learning company, and formerly the reasoning lead at Meta AI working on Llama, Galactica, and other models. He is joined by co-founder Chengxi Taylor. The talk is split into two parts: Ross covers the early days of language modeling (2020–2023) and RL efforts for LLMs, while Chengxi discusses the next frontiers in long-horizon agent scaling.

### Papers With Code and the Open-Weight Revolution [01:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=75s)
Ross traces his journey to the Papers With Code team, a London startup acquired by Meta in 2019. This small team transitioned into research, producing Galactica and then leading post-training for Llama 2 and Llama 3 after ChatGPT's release. Ross credits this tight-knit group as the origin of the open-weight revolution, emphasizing that small, aligned teams can achieve remarkable results even in the age of scaling.

### Galactica vs. ChatGPT: The Natural Experiment [02:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=136s)
Two weeks before ChatGPT launched, Meta released Galactica. Both were built on strong base models, but outcomes differed dramatically. Galactica shipped as a raw base model demo, exhibiting hallucinations and unpredictable behavior. ChatGPT, by contrast, had RLHF—a pipeline that made LLMs usable products for the first time. Ross frames this as a personal "natural experiment" proving RL provides critical value beyond a good base model.

### RLHF's Power and InstructGPT's Lesson [03:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=198s)
Ross highlights that RLHF's importance was visible even before ChatGPT. InstructGPT (2022) showed a 1-billion-parameter model with RLHF outperforming 175-billion-parameter models without it—two orders of magnitude fewer parameters with better results. The Galactica demo itself caused a public storm when users prompted it to generate absurd content (e.g., reports on the benefits of eating crushed glass), and the Meta association compounded the backlash.

### Galactica's Technical Achievements [04:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=259s)
Despite its public failure, Galactica was technically state-of-the-art. It outperformed PaLM, Chinchilla, and GPT-3.5 in scientific domains with significantly less compute. On math benchmarks it beat Chinchilla; on chain-of-thought reasoning, a 30-billion-parameter Galactica doubled PaLM 540B's performance (36% vs. 19%). Ross reiterates the paradox: a SOTA base model is not enough without RL post-training.

### Data Efficiency, Multi-Epoch Training, and Thinking Tokens [04:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=290s)
Galactica pioneered several contrarian ideas. It was the first major LM to crack data efficiency, using a 105-billion-token curated corpus versus the trillions of tokens used by Chinchilla. It was also the first major LM to empirically demonstrate multi-epoch training, challenging the then-consensus that more than one epoch was harmful. Most importantly, Galactica introduced "thinking tokens"—the idea that reasoning is an internal working memory process where the model spends inference compute inside designated tags before arriving at an answer.

### Early RL on Thinking Tags and the Bitter Lesson [06:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=381s)
After Galactica, Ross became obsessed with applying RL pressure to thinking tags. His team at Meta developed a recipe (unpublished, called RSP): continue pre-training Llama 2 on math/science data, then apply PPO with verifiable rewards, using a strong outcome reward model to initialize the value model. This achieved SOTA math results internally. However, they lacked the emergent reflective behavior (backtracking, self-correction) that later defined R1 and O1. When DeepSeek R1 emerged, Ross realized the difference was simply the bitter lesson: better base models, more RL compute, and bigger context windows. He also notes the sociological insight that having a GPT-4-level model first let OpenAI "see further" and discover more ideas.

### Founding GR and Transition to Long Horizons [08:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=536s)
Ross describes himself in 2024 as a "sad panda," defeated by ChatGPT and O1, but not deterred. Still convinced reasoning hadn't been solved, he founded GR (General Reasoning) to tackle truly big tasks. He then hands the stage to Chengxi.

### Long Horizon as a Mindset [09:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=567s)
Chengxi Taylor frames long-horizon tasks as a mindset, not merely an engineering problem. Solving humanity's greatest challenges—curing cancer, solving Millennium Prize problems, reaching Mars—requires patience measured in years. If AI is to drive that level of impact, long-horizon thinking is essential.

### The Context Window Problem and Compaction [10:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=629s)
Chengxi illustrates the gap using Fermat's Last Theorem: the mathematician spent over 10 years reading, writing scratchpad notes, and taking walks—potentially tens or hundreds of billions of tokens. Current context windows are only ~1 million tokens. Compaction (generate until the window fills, summarize, then continue) is one solution. RL can be applied to both compaction and the task simultaneously, but three issues arise: gradient variance scales with length, rewards are sparse creating credit assignment problems, and variable trajectory lengths complicate optimization.

### Value Models, Tools, and Infrastructure [11:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=660s)
To address these issues, Chengxi advocates for value models (critics), which reduce variance, fit compaction well at the trajectory level, encourage batch diversity, and enable bootstrapping (getting signal before episode end). The downside is added complexity—training a separate value model alongside the policy. He also mentions tool-based solutions: file system tools (scratchpads for reasoning), self-search tools (searching over previous trajectories), and archive tools (building on prior results, with safeguards against cheating by retrieving answers without thinking).

### The Kelly Benchmark: Frontier Models Lose Money [12:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=753s)
GR constructed the Kelly benchmark, featured on the front page of the Financial Times. Agents were given $100K to build ML models and trade Premier League football matches over a one-year horizon. All frontier models lost money. The public reaction was surprise that "AI is not as great as they thought." Chengxi attributes this failure to the industry's bias toward coding and procedural tasks (limited solutions, little creativity), insufficient focus on open-ended tasks, and lack of real-world simulation involving other players with different goals.

### Compute Optimization: Pipeline RL and Bootstrapping [14:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=878s)
Long-horizon reasoning demands careful compute allocation between training and inference. Pipeline RL trades off-policy staleness for GPU utilization: rather than waiting for all inference to finish before training, training begins while sequences are still being generated. From GR's experience, up to 8 steps off-policy is acceptable. However, multi-week inference eventually exceeds this limit, leaving GPUs idle. Bootstrapping via value models offers a workaround—generating expectations before episode end (like "dopamine in the human brain")—but introduces value model bias, creating a persistent trade-off.

### Open Review Platform and Closing Vision [16:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI&t=972s)
Chengxi mentions Open Review (openreview.ai), a GR platform hosting over 350 environments accessible via a single API endpoint, used internally and by frontier and new labs for RL. Summarizing the talk, he and Ross emphasize that long horizons require new thinking across algorithms, environments, and compute. Despite challenges and trade-offs, the mission is exciting because long-horizon work is essential to solving humanity's biggest problems. GR is a London-based AI research company inviting interested followers to join the journey.
