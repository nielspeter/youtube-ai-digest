## TL;DR
Sunny Rekhi, CTO of Forward Deployed Engineering at Decagon, explains how the company's forward deployed engineering function has evolved alongside its hypergrowth from 50 to 500 people. He details Decagon's approach to configuring AI customer service agents for enterprises, the critical importance of productizing custom work, and the mindset shift required to act as both an executor and an adviser to Fortune 20 clients.

## Key Takeaways
- Decagon is a 24/7 AI customer service agent that replaces traditional support channels with human-like, omni-channel, multilingual interactions, eventually expanding into proactive revenue-generating workflows.
- Forward deployed engineering at Decagon consists of two main functions: configuring the agentic brain for specific enterprises and acting as the frontline for customer product requests to feed back into the core product.
- At Decagon, forward deployed engineering is treated identically to product engineering—sharing the same bar, reporting structure, and often the same teams.
- During hypergrowth, the original "agent software engineer" role was split into two specialized lanes: "agent builders" (who configure agents largely within the UI) and "agent software engineers" (who build product features based on enterprise needs).
- With AI coding tools making implementation easier, the scarce skill is now exercising restraint to ensure solutions are architected to scale for future customers rather than relying on one-off patches.
- Defining success metrics early—ideally in writing—is crucial before beginning any deployment with a large enterprise.
- Staffing industry experts to deals within the same vertical compounds knowledge, increases credibility, and speeds up deployment times.
- Forward deployed engineers should view themselves as advisers, not just executors, leveraging cross-customer domain expertise to guide clients toward the highest-ROI automations.
- A guiding ethos is that any manual or custom work should eventually be upstreamed back into the product to become self-serve.
- Proving value as fast as possible is essential in enterprise deployments; complexity can be expanded after the initial relationship and trust are established.

### What Decagon Does [01:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=63s)
Sunny explains that Decagon is a 24/7 AI customer service agent. It replaces traditional, frustrating support experiences (like phone menus or delayed email replies) with immediate, human-like responses across channels. While they initially land to help deflect complex inbound support workflows that normally require humans, they eventually expand into revenue-generating workflows. He gives the example of Hertz, where Decagon moved from handling inbound support to proactively reaching out to customers for car lease renewals.

### Two Kinds of Forward Deployed Engineering [03:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=220s)
Sunny describes two types of forward deployed engineering at Decagon. The first is configuring the AI agent brain for an enterprise—training it like a human by defining instructions, brand tonality, user intents, and when to hand off to a human. The second is acting as the frontline for customer product requests. Forward deployed engineers must recognize when a request from Enterprise A will likely be needed by Enterprises B, C, and D, and ensure the solution is built to scale across all of them.

### Forward Deployment Equals Product Engineering [05:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=312s)
A key point Sunny emphasizes is that at Decagon, forward deployed engineering is identical to product engineering. There is no delineation between the two in terms of standards, reporting structure, or teams. When a Fortune 20 client expresses a pain point, it is treated as a product feature that needs to be prioritized. Sunny notes the company's hypergrowth from 50 to 500 people in a year and puts out a call for resumes.

### Role Evolution: From Generalists to Specialists [06:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=377s)
Historically, "agent software engineers" did everything—configuring the agent, integrating backend systems, and building platform features. At 500 people, the role was split into two specialized lanes. "Agent builders" are Decagon pros who configure agents largely within the UI, leveraging intuition about the underlying models. "Agent software engineers" focus on incorporating enterprise product requests back into the core platform.

### The Scarce Skill: Exercising Restraint [08:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=500s)
Sunny highlights that with AI coding tools making implementation fast, the true scarce skill is exercising restraint. Instead of quickly patching one-off requests for important customers, engineers must architect solutions that scale to future customers. Decagon's ethos is that agents should be owned by the customer, not turned into a brittle black box of prompts and patches.

### Defining Success and Industry Expertise [09:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=563s)
Sunny stresses the importance of defining success metrics early in the deal cycle—ideally in writing—to avoid miscommunication. This includes clarifying target metrics and support channels. He also shares that Decagon staffs industry experts to similar deals within a vertical (e.g., financial services), which compounds knowledge, builds credibility, and accelerates ramp-up times for new logos.

### Scaling Through Self-Serve and Upstreaming [11:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=689s)
Forward deployed engineers must think about empowering the rest of the business to solve problems. Decagon's ethos is that agents should be configurable via natural language. Sunny shares an example of CRM integrations: after building 25 custom integrations, they productized a self-serve solution. The mental model is to always upstream manual or custom work back into the product.

### Proving Value Fast [12:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=750s)
In enterprise deployments, especially with Fortune 500 companies, clients often request everything at once. Sunny advises proving value as fast as possible rather than embarking on a multi-month deployment. Once initial value is demonstrated, Decagon can expand into more complex workflows, treating the relationship as a multi-year partnership.

### Adviser, Not Just Executor [13:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=813s)
Sunny argues that forward deployed engineers should act as advisers, not just executors. Because they see the same problems across many customers, they have valuable domain expertise. At Decagon, they ingest historical support data to recommend the highest-ROI automations, which sometimes differs from what the customer initially requested.

### What Decagon Did Right and Conclusion [15:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=937s)
Sunny reflects on Decagon's success, attributing it to three things: moving very fast on customer asks, earning trust as advisers rather than just executors, and productizing custom work. As the company scaled to 500 people, they became more rigorous about designing systems, sharing knowledge across deployments, and ensuring the agent compounds in value with every customer interaction. He closes by reiterating the importance of configuring the agent, feeding insights back into the product, and scaling field knowledge for future customers.
