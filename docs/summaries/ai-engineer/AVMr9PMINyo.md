## TL;DR
Olive Song (MiniMax) and Dan (Together AI) discuss the development and serving of MiniMax M3, an open-source multimodal model with 1M context length and sparse attention. The conversation covers post-training challenges for long-horizon agentic tasks, the infrastructure required to serve shifting workloads from chat to agentic coding, and the collaborative dynamic between model creators and inference optimization teams.

## Key Takeaways
- MiniMax M3 is multimodal from scratch (text, image, video), with 1M context length and MiniMax sparse attention — a significant architectural departure from the M2 series.
- Open-sourcing the model aligns with MiniMax's mission of making intelligence accessible to everyone and enables community contributions, including inference optimizations by partners like Together AI.
- The partnership between MiniMax and Together AI began at a Las Vegas car event; Together AI now serves the lion's share of M3 token usage.
- Agentic workloads (hundreds of multi-turn tool calls, full codebase uploads) fundamentally change inference stack optimization compared to traditional chat workloads — affecting KV cache, routing, and kernel design.
- M3's multimodal training from scratch solved model collapse issues seen at other labs; attention maps show text tokens naturally attending to visual tokens, enabling better web development and visual understanding.
- Post-training for complex tasks (e.g., reproducing ICLR papers, kernel optimization) requires careful environment design, reward formulation, and RL algorithm tuning to handle long-horizon, GPU-constrained tasks.
- Together AI's "Parallel Kernel Bench" contains unsolved inference kernel problems — overfitting to it is encouraged because the resulting kernels directly accelerate real inference.
- KV cache management for long-context agentic workloads resembles building a distributed file system or large database — conceptually simple but operationally critical.
- Dan hopes that in 3 years, GPU utilization (currently ~10% FLOP utilization) will be dramatically improved and the open-source vs. closed frontier debate will be settled, with open models proving they can keep up.
- Self-evolution — where models build their own training harnesses and evaluations — is accelerating development speed and helping open-weight models close the gap with frontier labs.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Introductions and Roles [00:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=12s)
Dan is VP of Kernels at Together AI, leading inference and GPU optimization. Olive is the research lead of RL at MiniMax, responsible for final training and shipping of models — "everything before the inference."

### Why Open Source M3 [01:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=73s)
Olive explains that MiniMax believes the open source community is powerful. Open-sourcing aligns with their mission of making intelligence available to everyone, allows developers to contribute via feedback and PRs, and enables partners like Dan to optimize inference speed and serving.

### How the Partnership Came to Be [02:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=136s)
Dan recounts that the partnership started after a car event in Las Vegas where a MiniMax representative urged Together AI to serve their next model. Together AI had been serving MiniMax 2.5 and 2.7, and as M3 approached, they collaborated on architecture optimization and inference serving. Together AI now holds the lion's share of M3 token usage.

### Unique Capabilities and Hidden Gems of M3 [03:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=229s)
M3 is multimodal — understanding text, code, images, and video. Olive highlights applications in multimodal agents, computer use (navigating computers and using tools), and game development, which she calls a "hidden gem" of the model's capabilities.

### Post-Training for Kernel Bench, SVG Bench, and OS World [05:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=324s)
Olive emphasizes that data and problem formulation are critical. For kernel optimization tasks, they design environments for reinforcement learning where the model can iteratively optimize kernels and improve performance on its own.

### Parallel Kernel Bench [05:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=356s)
Dan introduces Together AI's "Parallel Kernel Bench," which contains unsolved kernel problems that would speed up model inference. He notes that overfitting to this benchmark is welcome — the resulting kernels will be used directly to accelerate real inference. The moderator agrees: if researchers put out useful benchmarks and models overfit to them, that produces genuinely useful models.

### Behind the Scenes of the Inference Stack [07:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=448s)
Dan describes the process: Together AI gets early model details (e.g., MiniMax sparse attention), then starts writing kernels, benchmarking, and determining what needs modification or creation from scratch. Day zero focuses on quality and user experience. After launch, they work through a long list of KV cache, attention kernel, and quantization optimizations, with performance improving week over week — sometimes even overnight.

### Agentic Workloads Changing the Inference Stack [09:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=571s)
Dan explains that agentic, turn-based workloads affect KV cache, prompting, and routing decisions. Unlike chat workloads with a few-thousand-token system prompt, coding agents upload entire codebases — a fundamentally different optimization and kernel challenge.

### Multimodal Training from Scratch [10:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=634s)
Olive explains that M3 was trained multimodal from step zero, solving the model collapse problem that other labs experienced. Attention maps show text tokens naturally attending to visual tokens, meaning the modalities are inherently combined. This enables better web development — the model can look at a website, understand its appearance, and optimize accordingly, including during reinforcement learning.

### Architecture and Optimization Priorities [11:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=697s)
M3 introduced multimodality, 1M context length, and sparse attention simultaneously. When asked how to prioritize among a thousand possible optimizations, Dan says you focus on "a thousand and one things" — leaving no stone unturned and pushing on every edge you can find.

### Lessons Transferable Across Models [12:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=758s)
Dan confirms that lessons from optimizing one model transfer to others. Sparse attentions are now popular across models; while MiniMax's sparse attention differs from DeepSeek's and others, the optimization and kernel-writing processes share similarities. Dan notes he's been thinking about these problems since his PhD and finds validation in seeing them deployed at scale.

### Training for Long-Horizon Tasks (12-Hour ICLR Replication) [14:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=852s)
Olive describes training M3 to replicate ICLR papers and optimize kernel frameworks as exciting but tricky — these are long-horizon tasks with GPU hardware constraints. The key is environment design, data, reward formulation, and adapting RL algorithms for more efficient training so emergent capabilities appear through RL iterations.

### Evaluation Over Long-Horizon Runs [15:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=914s)
Olive explains that models can submit multiple times during a task, with each submission evaluated. They check for "hacking" — whether the model is genuinely improving performance or gaming the evaluation. They also design internal evaluations, such as using the model to speed up their own development and building evaluations closely tied to their actual work.

### KV Cache for Long-Context Agentic Workloads [16:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=976s)
Dan compares KV cache management to recreating a distributed file system or building a very large database. It's conceptually simple — where to store the cache, how to detect if you've seen it before, how to fetch and transfer it — but operationally critical. He jokes that it's the kind of thing you should have learned in undergrad but most people skipped that class.

### Looking Ahead 3 Years [17:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVMr9PMINyo&t=1037s)
Dan hopes that in 3 years, GPU utilization (currently ~10% FLOP utilization, citing SpaceX) will be dramatically improved and that the open-source vs. closed frontier debate will be settled. He sees models like M3, GLM, and Kimi proving that open-source can catch up and isn't far behind. Olive notes that 3 years ago she wasn't even in the industry, but she can see how models have been accelerating development speed, which is how open-weight models can close the gap with frontier labs.
