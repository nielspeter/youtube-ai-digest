## TL;DR
Natalie Meurer, Head of Agent Engineering at Sierra, traces the evolution of forward deployed engineering (FDE) from its origins at Palantir in 2008 through its many permutations—DevOps, data integration, custom solutioning, and customer enablement—to argue that the "dirty secret" of FDE is that it no longer has a coherent definition. However, as code becomes cheap to produce and software pricing shifts toward outcomes, the customer accountability at the heart of FDE is becoming central to all engineering disciplines, making "everything is forward deployed engineering" the defining trend of the AI era.

## Key Takeaways
- **The "dirty secret" of FDE is that it doesn't exist as a single, coherent role**—it has grown to encompass so many responsibilities that the term has lost precise meaning, yet it remains the "hottest job in AI."
- **FDE originated at Palantir around 2008 as essentially DevOps**, focused on platform stability and on-prem deployments, with engineers physically located with customers.
- **The role evolved through distinct "vintages"**: platform stability (2008), data integration and ontology modeling (2012), custom solutioning with dashboards (2016), and customer enablement (2020 onward).
- **Palantir's success in producing founder-alums stems from FDE being a generalist training ground**, exposing engineers to the full stack of customer-facing engineering work.
- **Today's FDE job postings are comically broad**, effectively asking for a staff engineer with years of sales, solution architecture, and even teaching experience combined.
- **The one continuity across all eras of FDE is customer accountability**—whether for DevOps, enablement, or custom solutioning, the engineer owns the customer's outcome.
- **As coding agents make code cheaper to produce, FDEs can move beyond prototyping to building end-to-end solutions**, dramatically increasing their impact on product development.
- **Product engineering and FDE are converging**: good product engineers must now think about customers, and good FDEs must think about the product.
- **Software pricing is shifting from seat-based to outcome-based**, driven by AI agents that can be directly attributed to business outcomes (e.g., resolving a customer inquiry, closing a sale).
- **Meurer's 2024 framing of "agent engineering" as a distinct subdiscipline was incomplete**—she now believes everything is trending toward FDE, with product, agent, AI, solutions, and customer engineering all converging around customer accountability and outcome delivery.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Introduction and Audience Poll [00:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=1s)
Meurer opens by surveying the post-lunch audience for forward deployed engineers and those hiring for the role, noting a mix of both. She introduces herself as Head of Agent Engineering at Sierra, where she's been for about two years, and frames the talk as less about Sierra and more about the history and "dirty secret" of forward deployed engineering as a discipline.

### Meurer's Background [01:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=63s)
She traces her unconventional path: starting as a policy nerd at Georgetown's School of Foreign Service, learning to code, and joining Palantir in 2016 where she worked on the privacy team as both an infrastructure engineer and forward deployed engineer with law enforcement and defense. After Palantir she went to Stanford for an MBA, then joined Sierra in 2024, initially on the deployment team—a name she disliked. In July 2024 she wrote an article proposing "agent engineering" as a subdiscipline of AI engineering with the same customer accountability as FDE but as its own domain. Since then, she's observed the discourse evolving further, with "harness engineering" emerging as a near-subset of agent engineering, prompting this talk's exploration of what these terms actually mean.

### The Dirty Secret [03:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=188s)
Meurer reveals the thesis upfront: forward deployed engineering "doesn't exist" as a coherent concept because it has been stretched to describe so many things that it "sort of means nothing at this point." Yet it remains the hottest job in AI. She promises to convince the audience that this is both true and, importantly, that it doesn't matter.

### FDE Origins at Palantir (2008) [03:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=219s)
She begins the historical trace by pulling up Palantir job postings from 2016—when she joined—which led with location rather than skills. "Forward-deployed" literally meant sitting with customers on the ground, partly because Palantir's ecosystem was built around on-prem deployments. The prototypical image is of a fresh graduate parachuting in to fix runtime issues, but in practice the early job was essentially DevOps. Her onboarding project at Palantir was deploying software on an EC2 instance, and much of the work involved responding to 2 a.m. customer emails about someone unplugging a server.

### Data Integration Era (2012) [05:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=312s)
By 2012, the Palantir platform had stabilized, but the data integration software was useless without integrated data—like a movie theater playing nothing. FDEs now had a dual role: DevOps plus data integration. They needed to deeply understand the customer's environment to model data into what Palantir called an "ontology," a taxonomy for that data, mostly written in Java variants. Palantir's own client was built in Java Swing.

### Custom Solutions and Foundry (2016–2020) [07:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=437s)
Around 2016, FDEs began building custom solutions to known problems, often using Slate, an internal drag-and-drop builder similar to Retool that mapped UI components to data sources. Because FDEs understood the data best, they were best positioned to make it useful. This era gave way to Foundry, Palantir's main platform, focused on going "from data to decision-making." A key insight: dashboards that don't write back to data sources decay over time and lose usefulness. By 2020, with Palantir approaching IPO, the company needed a model that didn't require shipping engineers to Finland or Canberra indefinitely, so FDEs began enabling customers to do the work themselves—exemplified by the Airbus Skywise partnership, which empowered thousands of Airbus engineers on Foundry. Palantir later launched AIP (AIFTE) to leverage LLMs for similar work, with Alex Karp running AIP bootcamps where FDEs and deployment strategists taught customers to use the software.

### The FDE Vintage Concept [09:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=559s)
Meurer emphasizes that these were not discrete eras—the total jobs to be done actually grew over time, stacking rather than replacing. She proposes the concept of "FDE vintages," suggesting interviewers ask candidates which vintage they are: 2008 (platform stability with hints of panic), 2012, 2016, 2020, or today. Palantir's success in producing founder-alums is precisely because FDE trained people across all these facets, making it an exceptional generalist training ground.

### FDE Everywhere in 2026 [10:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=621s)
Today, FDE is everywhere: Google announced FDE-like "customer engineers" for GCP, OpenAI created a new unit with massive investment for corporate AI support, and Aaron Levie and others publicly champion the role. Yet the work isn't one thing—if you merged all current FDE job postings, you'd get an impossible composite: a staff engineer for eight years, with six years of sales experience, four years as a solution architect, and ideally prior teaching experience. The dirty secret is that FDE either doesn't exist or exists too much.

### The Continuity: Customer Accountability [11:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=712s)
Across all vintages and facets—DevOps, enablement, custom solutioning, data integration—the one continuity is customer accountability. Meurer then poses the broader question: what happens to engineering when code becomes cheap to produce, when you can fire off a prompt to a background agent and get something great back?

### Convergence of Product and FDE [12:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=774s)
FDEs can now do far more than talk to customers and prototype—they can build end-to-end solutions with coding agents, making them far more impactful in product development. At Sierra, she observes the lines blurring: product engineering is becoming more client-facing, and good engineers in either role must think about both product and customer together. FDE is effectively getting larger, stacking more skills, while product and infra engineers must also think about deployment (DevOps). A parallel shift, highlighted by Emergence Capital and Sierra's head of go-to-market ops Elliot Greenwald, is in how software is sold.

### Outcome-Based Pricing [13:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=836s)
Meurer maps software pricing along two axes: attribution of outcome to the product, and agency/autonomy to achieve the outcome. Seat-based pricing (low attribution, low agency) has dominated for ages. Usage-based pricing (e.g., paying OpenAI or Anthropic) has higher agency but still hard outcome attribution. AI-driven customer experience moves into the top-right: outcome-based pricing where you pay for whether a sale was made or a customer inquiry resolved. She predicts most pricing in this market will move to outcome-based. With FDEs contributing more to product and pricing shifting to outcomes, the question becomes: how do you guarantee the outcome? That, she argues, is forward deployed engineering.

### Revised Vision: Everything Is FDE [15:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=931s)
Meurer revisits her 2024 framing of agent engineering as a distinct subset of AI engineering and concedes it wasn't the whole story. Agent engineering is a flavor of FDE, heavily focused on outcomes. Today she believes everything is trending toward forward deployed engineering—product engineering, agent engineering, AI engineering, solutions engineering, and customer engineering all involve building, deploying, and enabling on behalf of customers, with outcomes that should be priced accordingly. FDE as a concept may lack a coherent definition, but it is precisely what enables those outcomes. She closes with "forward deployed engineering is dead—and long live forward deployed engineering," and notes Sierra is hiring across FDE roles.
