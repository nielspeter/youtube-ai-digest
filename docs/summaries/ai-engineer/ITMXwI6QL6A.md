# How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Ramp — Leo Mehr

## TL;DR
Leo Mehr, Director of Engineering at Ramp, shares two core principles that define Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE) at Ramp: "always be scoping" (rigorously validating customer requests before building) and "scale with tokens" (automating the FDE workflow using AI agents). He argues that mastering both is essential—scoping without AI automation leaves you vulnerable to competitors, while AI automation without scoping produces "token maxing slop."

## Key Takeaways
- FDE at Ramp lives within the engineering organization and focuses on making the core product and agentic features work well for large enterprise customers.
- An FDE's job is not to blindly say yes to customers; it is to ask probing questions, validate urgency, identify workarounds, and build the *right* thing.
- Scoping must include looking beyond a single request to see if other prospects or customers would benefit from the same feature.
- Basic assumptions—such as which mobile platform a customer mandates—must be validated upfront to avoid wasted engineering effort.
- FDEs must "scale with tokens" by breaking their workflow into stages (context gathering, scoping, spec writing, implementation) and replacing each with agents.
- Ramp automated the intake of FDE requests via a Notion-based agent that conducts multi-round questioning with submitters, saving roughly 20% of scoping time.
- The hardest remaining challenge is the "middle" of the pipeline—turning raw context into a well-shaped spec—and getting agents the right product context.
- FDEs retain responsibility for taste and judgment over the final output, even as agents handle more of the execution.
- Scoping and token-scaling are complementary: without scoping, automation produces low-quality output; without automation, you fall behind agent-native competitors.

## Detailed Breakdown

### What FDE Means at Ramp [00:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ITMXwI6QL6A&t=32s)
Mehr introduces himself as a Director of Engineering who joined Ramp 2.5 years ago when FDE was just two engineers; his org is now ~30 engineers across four teams. He clarifies that FDE at Ramp is not a "boss mode" of technical go-to-market roles. Instead, it lives within the engineering organization and focuses on making Ramp's core product and new agentic features work for the largest enterprise customers, helping Ramp win upmarket.

### Principle One: Always Be Scoping [02:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ITMXwI6QL6A&t=130s)
Mehr pushes back on the idea that an FDE should simply say yes to every customer request. Saying yes indiscriminately would produce chaotic results—like "horses with rockets strapped to their legs" instead of polished Waymos. The goal is to help customers succeed while delivering good software, which requires building the right thing.

### The SAP Integration Example [03:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ITMXwI6QL6A&t=191s)
A common scenario: on a Friday night, an enterprise sales rep demands an SAP S/4HANA integration to close a strategic deal. The default engineering reflex is to hunt for API docs, but a well-trained FDE pauses and asks what is actually driving the urgency—sometimes it is the rep's end-of-quarter quota pressure rather than genuine customer urgency. The FDE asks who will use the integration, whether workarounds exist, whether the customer can hit Ramp's API instead, and whether other prospects would benefit from the same work.

### The Mobile Reimbursement Lesson [04:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ITMXwI6QL6A&t=285s)
In Ramp's early days, a large enterprise customer needed a mobile reimbursement feature. The mobile team was swamped, so two FDEs taught themselves iOS and Android development and shipped the feature on both platforms. When they asked the customer for their Android beta user list, the customer revealed they mandate iOS devices for all employees. The painful lesson: validate even the most basic assumptions upfront.

### Principle Two: Scale with Tokens [06:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ITMXwI6QL6A&t=378s)
Even teams that master scoping will fall behind unless they scale with model capabilities. Mehr argues that knowledge workers must constantly reinvent their jobs by figuring out how to have models and agents do their work. For FDEs, this means examining the entire lifecycle—gathering context, scoping, writing specs, implementing—and replacing each stage with agents where possible.

### Automating Request Intake with Notion Agents [07:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ITMXwI6QL6A&t=471s)
Ramp has an internal Slack channel called "FDE requests" where account managers, solutions engineers, and sales reps post blockers for large prospects or customers. Requests are logged in Notion, but quality varies wildly—from detailed write-ups to one-liners. Previously, FDEs manually read and triaged each request. Ramp built a V1 Notion agent that simply took the request and asked a couple of clarifying questions. Reply latency dropped from hours or days to seconds, and reps immediately engaged with the agent. A later iteration features a friendly penguin mascot and conducts multiple rounds of back-and-forth questioning until it deems the request ready to generate a spec, saving an estimated 20% of scoping time.

### The Pipeline and the Hard Middle [10:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ITMXwI6QL6A&t=624s)
The request-intake agent addresses only the first stage of the FDE pipeline. The last stage—turning a well-shaped spec into a working product—is also becoming tractable as frontier models can one-shot medium-sized features. The difficult "middle" remains: converting raw, unformed context into a well-shaped spec. Mehr is excited about investing in building out this "factory" of agents for each pipeline step.

### Future Challenges: Context, Evals, and Judgment [11:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ITMXwI6QL6A&t=686s)
Looking 6–12 months ahead, Mehr sees FDE spending most of its time on applied AI problems: ensuring the agent harness runs smoothly, validating output quality with evals and rubrics, incorporating human feedback, and—most critically—getting agents the right context. Product knowledge that lives in a PM's head is hard to capture; existing Notion docs and help articles only go so far. Skills, memories, and tools all matter. Yet FDEs still retain responsibility for taste and judgment over the final output.

### Closing: You Need Both [12:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ITMXwI6QL6A&t=747s)
Mehr ties the two principles together. If you automate without scoping, you get a "token maxing slop cannon." If you scope well but do not invest in agent automation, agent-native competitors will overtake you. The future of FDE requires both: always be scoping and scale with tokens.
