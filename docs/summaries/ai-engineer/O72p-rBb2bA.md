## TL;DR
Akele Reed and Dave Revere of SonderMind present "Sonder," a clinically grounded AI mental health coach, and detail the architectural and evaluative frameworks that make it safe. They emphasize building modular, separate LLM-as-judge guardrails, calibrating for *correct* rather than *more* triggers, and creating a clinician-driven learning loop that translates real-world edge cases into CI-gated evals. They also announce the open-sourcing of 300 clinically reviewed guardrail scenarios to establish a shared safety baseline for the industry.

## Key Takeaways
- **Purpose-built AI:** General-purpose LLMs are over-calibrated and inappropriate for mental health care; SonderMind built "Sonder" specifically to handle sensitive mental health support safely.
- **Modular Guardrails:** Input and output guardrails are kept as separate LLM-as-judge calls, making them more robust, harder to jailbreak, and easier to evaluate independently, despite trade-offs in latency and cost.
- **Correct vs. More Triggers:** Over-calibration (false positives) can feel like a "door slam" to vulnerable users. The goal is to trigger only on genuine safety risks by understanding clinical nuance (e.g., past trauma vs. active crisis).
- **The Sentence Underneath the Sentence:** Direct keyword matching or moderation APIs fail to catch indirect, coded language (e.g., "I packed a box today just to feel what it would be like to be gone"). Context and implication matter.
- **Clinician-Driven Learning Loops:** The system relies on licensed clinicians to annotate real conversation traces. These annotations are converted into typed evals that gate releases in CI.
- **Human as the Center Node:** Safety systems must be reviewed and tested by subject matter experts. The system isn't deciding what "correct" is in clinical edge cases—the licensed professional is.
- **Open-Sourced Datasets:** SonderMind open-sourced 200 input and 100 output clinically reviewed guardrail scenarios to provide a shared baseline for anyone building in the mental health AI space.
- **Turning Off Built-in Guardrails:** To build their own custom guardrails, SonderMind had to turn off the default safety filters provided by frontier model APIs (like OpenAI and Anthropic), as they filtered everything out.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Introduction and Context [00:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O72p-rBb2bA&t=1s)
Akele Reed introduces the talk, issuing a content warning for sensitive topics like suicide and domestic violence. She explains that SonderMind is a mental health care company that matches individuals with therapists and psychiatrists, with a focus on access and outcomes. Reed introduces "Sonder," a clinically grounded AI coach built to address the gap in mental health support, noting that general-purpose LLMs have tragically failed in this space. Sonder acts as a 24/7 support tool and a front door to human providers when necessary.

### Guardrail Architecture and Modularity [03:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O72p-rBb2bA&t=186s)
Reed details the technical harness built around Sonder. It features input and output guardrails that "sandwich" the core AI. Input guardrails assess user messages for necessary intervention, while output guardrails monitor the AI's response and the overall conversation trajectory. Modularity was a key design principle, allowing the team to iterate on the core without compromising safety. The guardrails are kept as separate LLM-as-judge calls to make them harder to circumvent via prompt engineering, accepting latency and cost trade-offs due to the sensitivity of the use case.

### Correct Triggers vs. More Triggers [06:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O72p-rBb2bA&t=360s)
General-purpose LLMs are too conservative, often triggering guardrails inappropriately. Reed explains that in mental health, an inappropriate guardrail feels like a "door slam" to someone in a vulnerable moment. The goal is *correct* triggers, not more of them. She walks through three synthetic scenarios: an active domestic violence crisis (Sonder surfaces resources and disengages), past trauma uncertainty (Sonder surfaces resources but continues talking), and relationship challenges (passes through to normal conversation).

### Trusting the Guardrails and Indirect Language [09:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O72p-rBb2bA&t=555s)
Dave Revere takes over, using a personal anecdote about his son to illustrate that "the words aren't always the message." He presents a challenging example: "I packed a box today just to feel what it would be like to be gone." Revere notes that simple regex, verbose prompt instructions, or broad moderation APIs fail to catch this clinical nuance. The signal is in the implication and context—the "sentence underneath the sentence."

### Clinician Annotations and CI Integration [11:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O72p-rBb2bA&t=679s)
Revere explains how the system handles these edge cases. The conversation is traced, and a clinician reviews and annotates the trace using a provided rubric. This annotation—capturing the conversation input, expected result, expected observation, and metadata—is transformed by a script into a typed eval normalized into their schema. Once committed, a clinician's judgment lives in CI, ensuring every prompt, model, or guardrail change is scored against what the clinician taught them.

### Calibration and Open-Sourced Datasets [13:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O72p-rBb2bA&t=807s)
Revere warns against over-calibration, which can prevent people from getting needed care. He outlines three design choices: clinicians own the definition of "good," labeled scenarios ask concrete questions (did the right category trigger at the right time?), and benchmarks focus on real human needs and failure modes rather than perfection. To establish a shared industry baseline, SonderMind open-sourced 300 clinically reviewed guardrail scenarios (200 input, 100 output). Revere concludes by thanking the team, especially clinician Caroline Collie, and the audience.

### Q&A: Model Guardrails and False Positives [18:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O72p-rBb2bA&t=1122s)
During the brief Q&A, an audience member asks how SonderMind handles the built-in guardrails of frontier models (like OpenAI and Anthropic) that refuse sensitive prompts, and what trade-off they choose between false positives and false negatives. Reed confirms they had to turn off the built-in API guardrails from day one because general-purpose LLMs are over-calibrated and filtered everything. She notes that while over-calibration is a compassionate choice by providers, SonderMind works to make that margin much smaller to achieve more correct triggers.
