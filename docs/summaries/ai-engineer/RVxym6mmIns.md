## TL;DR
Jia Wu, a deployed engineering lead at Cognition, explains how the forward-deployed engineering (FDE) motion at Cognition is central to making Devin valuable in the enterprise. FDEs embed deeply with customers, map Devin's capabilities to high-leverage business problems, measure real ROI, and feed field insights back into the product roadmap—ultimately delivering step-function increases in engineering productivity.

## Key Takeaways
- Cognition's FDE model is built on maximizing the overlap between Devin's product capabilities and real enterprise problems.
- Coding itself is considered a "mostly solved problem"; the harder challenges are testing, reviewing, deploying, and maintaining code at scale.
- FDEs spend roughly half their day in customer calls and half doing hands-on keyboard work, giving them high-fidelity signal on what matters strategically.
- A core differentiator is making the entire organization faster—not just individual engineers—which single-point tools (CLIs, IDEs) can't achieve alone.
- Cognition measures impact through engineering hours generated, delivery timeline compression, and raw PR throughput.
- Case studies show ~150% effective headcount increase, ~82% reduction in delivery timelines, and ~2x PR output compared to pre-Devin baselines.
- Public examples include Nubank (ETL migration with 50 engineers, delivered in 1/3 the timeline) and a major Latin American bank (COBOL/JCL tax-ID migration at half the effort).
- FDEs are hired for "T-shaped" profiles: broad skills across people, business, process, and technology, with deep spikes in at least one area (e.g., product management, founder experience, deep technical expertise).
- Two guiding mantras: relentless curiosity for *why* a problem matters to the business, and relentless tie-in to the customer as lifeblood.
- The FDE role blurs sales, post-sales, and product—everybody is go-to-market, and the ultimate goal is customer success at all costs.

### Internal Impact and Adoption Growth [02:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=126s)
Internally at Cognition over the last six months, despite being behind on hiring, the team shipped nearly an order of magnitude more high-quality PRs by using their own agent. Externally, adoption is growing parabolically as companies deploy Devin across multiple use cases and scenarios.

### The FDE Framework: Product-Market Fit [02:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=158s)
Wu frames the FDE motion using two overlapping circles: one representing the product domain and the other representing customer problems. The intersection is product-market fit. The forward-deployed motion aims to maximize that overlap—ensuring Cognition's products solve real enterprise problems.

### Understanding the Problem Space [03:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=219s)
FDEs must deeply understand the problem space. Wu argues that coding is a mostly solved problem—models are good enough that with sufficient context engineering, you can generate the code blocks you need. The real challenge is the rest of the software lifecycle: testing, reviewing, deploying, and maintaining code, especially in legacy codebases. Building from zero to one is the easy part; ongoing maintenance is where business value compounds.

### Mapping Devin's Capabilities to Customer Problems [05:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=311s)
Deploying agents without specific direction is just "token maxing"—wasting spend with no tangible outcomes. FDEs spend four to five hours a day in customer calls to understand which strategic initiatives are highest-leverage, then set up the agent to automate those workflows. Measuring ROI remains an unsolved, ambiguous problem—Wu jokes that whoever solves it will be a "$5 trillion market cap" company.

### Closing the Feedback Loop [06:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=403s)
Solving the customer's problem is only half the equation. FDEs must also feed insights back to the product team to de-risk the roadmap. The field provides the highest-fidelity evaluation set: recurring problem shapes, decisions about whether workarounds should become features, and signal on what to build next. This feedback loop makes each subsequent deployment better than the last.

### FDE Personas and Hiring [08:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=497s)
Cognition hires T-shaped FDEs with broad skills across people, business, process, and technology, plus deep spikes in one area. Examples include product managers with strong product-design intuition (increasingly important as the cost of software engineering trends toward zero) and founder-engineers with deep technical expertise. Wu notes that business sense can be learned, but technical depth is harder to teach on the job.

### Two Guiding Mantras [09:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=590s)
1. **Relentless curiosity for why**: Always asking why a problem matters to the business and whether it can be communicated back to improve the roadmap for everyone.
2. **Relentless tie-in to the customer**: Customers are the lifeblood; making them successful is the only way to survive and become the obvious enterprise partner.

### From Token Maxing to Measurable Outcomes [10:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=621s)
A year or two ago, the KPI was maximizing token usage—budgets were subsidized, and you could run anything. Now the focus has shifted to measurable delivery outcomes. Wu draws a key distinction: making an individual engineer 10x faster is valuable, but making the entire organization 10x faster—including non-technical people—is where true partnership value unlocks. Single-point tools (CLIs, IDEs) fail at this because they don't reach across the whole organization.

### Proof Points: Engineering Hours [11:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=712s)
Cognition measures "sessions" (agent traces/trajectories) and derives metrics on productive engineering hours generated. In one three-month embedded engagement, they delivered the equivalent of ~150% additional headcount—like having 150 extra coworkers on a project.

### Proof Points: Delivery Timeline Compression [12:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=774s)
Across agile delivery metrics (tickets, sprints, milestones), customers saw an ~82% reduction in delivery timelines after full Devin activation—compressing time-to-value significantly.

### Proof Points: PR Throughput [13:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=805s)
Looking at raw PRs, Devin delivered nearly double the PR output that engineers achieved with single-point tools before adopting the agent harness.

### Public Case Studies [14:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=868s)
- **Nubank**: An ETL migration staffed by 50 engineers was completed in roughly one-third the timeline using Devin autonomously.
- **Major Latin American bank**: Migration of a tax-identification system involving legacy COBOL and JCL—delivered with half the expected effort.
- **Bilt**: Achieved an order-of-magnitude improvement in PR acceptance rate, generating the weekly output of 10+ engineers. Wu notes Bilt's engineers are exceptional, making the multiplier even more impressive.

### Closing: Mission, Culture, and Call to Action [15:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVxym6mmIns&t=957s)
Wu wraps by emphasizing that Cognition's core values extend externally, not just internally. The team goes all-in for customer success—citing an example of deploying someone to Brazil for 10 months to live next to a customer. There's no ego: engineering practices that need fixing are flagged and fed back to product. Everybody is go-to-market, every second counts, and the mission is customer success at all costs. She invites anyone interested in FDE at Cognition to reach out.
