## TL;DR

Ari Morcos, CEO and co-founder of DatologyAI, argues that data quality is the single most underleveraged compute multiplier in AI model training. As compute becomes increasingly scarce and expensive, investing in rigorous data curation—cleaning, curating, creating, and composing data—can deliver performance equivalent to having 10x–100x more compute, making frontier-quality models achievable for far less cost.

## Key Takeaways

- Compute availability has become severely constrained, with H100 prices rising ~40% from their lows and reasoning models consuming 8x more tokens than non-reasoning models, with another 5x increase projected.
- Data quality acts as a compute multiplier by steepening the learning curve: better data yields dramatically better performance for the same compute budget, or equivalent performance at a fraction of the cost.
- There is no single universal "golden dataset"—optimal data is always relative to the target output tasks and must be diverse, relevant, and correctly mixed.
- DatologyAI's approach centers on four "C's": Clean (heuristic filters, benchmark decontamination), Curate (quality classifiers, taxonomy, redundancy reduction, task distribution matching), Create (synthetic data via rephrasing), and Compose (sequencing data across training stages).
- In vision-language models, curation alone delivered ~14 absolute percentage point improvements and matched a Qwen 3.5 4B model using 145x less training compute.
- Curated data also produces more concise model responses, yielding ~35x fewer inference flops per correct answer compared to comparable models.
- For multilingual models, using only 8% multilingual tokens with proper curation matched Qwen 3 performance with ~8x less compute, and curating English data surprisingly improved non-English performance through cross-lingual transfer.
- Synthetic data via "rephrasing"—converting high-quality source documents into hundreds of template formats (e.g., true/false questions)—avoids model collapse and dramatically increases diversity without needing the rephrasing model to understand the content.
- Thompson Reuters saw legal capabilities rise ~5 percentage points with less than 1% of the pre-training budget, with no catastrophic forgetting, and post-training gains nearly tripled when applied to a well-curated mid-trained model.
- RCI trained a frontier-competitive general-purpose model on 17 trillion curated tokens for under $20 million total, demonstrating that custom high-performance models are achievable at far lower costs than commonly believed.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Introduction and the Compute Scarcity Problem [00:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_PdK6x7PQNM&t=13s)

Ari Morcos introduces himself as CEO and co-founder of DatologyAI, a company whose name means "the science or study of data." He frames the talk around data quality as an overlooked compute multiplier. He describes how compute availability has become extremely scarce over the past six months: H100 prices reversed their multi-year decline and are now ~40% above their late-2024 lows. Reasoning models use 8x more tokens than non-reasoning models, with another 5x increase projected within a year. This has led to real-world constraints—Google capped Meta's Gemini API usage due to inference limits, and OpenAI began selling token futures to guarantee future capacity. Morcos notes it's no longer implausible that frontier API access could be limited simply due to insufficient inference supply.

### Data Quality as a Compute Multiplier [02:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_PdK6x7PQNM&t=137s)

Morcos presents a simple schematic: model performance on the y-axis plotted against data (or equivalently compute, time, or dollars) on the x-axis. Better data quality transforms the curve, yielding dramatically better performance for the same compute budget—or the same performance at a much smaller budget. The core objective is to maximize marginal information gain per data point: finding the data that teaches the model the most. He emphasizes there is no universal optimal dataset; data must be relevant to target use cases, diverse enough to ensure robustness, and correctly mixed across sources.

### The Four C's: DatologyAI's Curation Framework [03:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_PdK6x7PQNM&t=230s)

DatologyAI positions itself as an "oil refinery for data"—they don't source new tokens but improve existing ones from public, proprietary, and licensed datasets. Their methodology has four pillars: **Clean** (heuristic filters like those from the Gopher paper, removing low-quality documents, and rigorous benchmark decontamination with low n-gram thresholds); **Curate** (quality classifiers, taxonomy across topics, redundancy reduction, upsampling/downsampling based on quality and relevance, and task distribution matching); **Create** (synthetic data via rephrasing to increase size and diversity); and **Compose** (sequencing and combining data across training stages, including continuous curricula).

### Research Foundation and Vision-Language Model Results [06:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_PdK6x7PQNM&t=387s)

Morcos references his foundational paper "Beyond Scaling Laws" (best paper at NeurIPS), which demonstrated that proper data selection can bend the scaling laws themselves by changing the exponent—avoiding wasted compute on redundant data. For vision-language models (VLMs), DatologyAI curated the ~25 billion-token Mammoth dataset used for fusion adapter training. Results showed ~14 absolute percentage point improvement through curation alone, and they matched Qwen 3.5 4B's performance using 145x less training compute. Curated models also produced more concise responses, achieving similar accuracy with ~35x fewer inference flops per correct answer.

### Multilingual Model Performance [09:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_PdK6x7PQNM&t=573s)

Morcos addresses the bias in internet data toward English and Western use cases, with implications for global fairness and model usability. Using only 8% multilingual tokens (with most languages receiving at most 6 billion tokens), DatologyAI's curated models matched Qwen 3 performance with ~8x less compute. He highlights a scientifically interesting finding: curating English data alone improved non-English performance through cross-lingual transfer, with the magnitude of transfer correlated with linguistic similarity to English. The reverse effect (non-English curation helping English) also appeared, though smaller. He also shows that scaling behavior from small curated models (trained on 1 trillion tokens) can predict the performance of much larger models (trained on 17 trillion tokens), enabling teams to de-risk large runs with 50–100x less compute.

### Synthetic Data via Rephrasing [12:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_PdK6x7PQNM&t=727s)

DatologyAI pioneered a synthetic data approach called "rephrasing," now considered table stakes. The method takes source documents and converts them into hundreds of template formats (e.g., true/false questions). Because all information derives from the source document, model collapse is not a concern. The rephrasing model doesn't need to understand the content—it only needs to accurately transform the document into the target format. Critical to success is selecting high-quality documents for rephrasing; random selection yields poor results. This approach can be applied to any proprietary data within a customer's own environment.

### Thompson Reuters Case Study: Mid-Training and Post-Training Synergy [14:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_PdK6x7PQNM&t=848s)

Thomson Reuters partnered with DatologyAI to build better legal models. After 100 billion tokens of mid-training (less than 1% of the pre-training budget), legal capabilities rose ~5 percentage points on LegalBench with no catastrophic forgetting—general capabilities also improved. The key was including mostly data representative of the pre-training distribution alongside domain-specific data. Even more striking: when Thomson Reuters applied their existing post-training harness to the mid-trained model, post-training gains nearly tripled compared to applying the same post-training to a default instruction-tuned model. Morcos argues this shows training stages should be viewed synergistically, not as isolated handoffs.

### RCI Case Study: Frontier-Competitive Model for Under $20 Million [16:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_PdK6x7PQNM&t=972s)

RCI trained "Trendy Large," a fully open, US-made model on 17 trillion curated public tokens with no proprietary data and no closed-model usage. The model is competitive with the open frontier, matching GLM5 and Kimi on many tasks and outperforming Claude on some. Critically, RCI had never trained a model before mid-2024. Total cost across salaries, compute, R&D, and all repetitions was under $20 million. Morcos uses this to counter the narrative that custom models require hundreds of millions of dollars, noting that narrow-domain models can be built for high six figures to low millions.

### Closing Message and Hiring [17:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_PdK6x7PQNM&t=1065s)

Morcos summarizes: focus on maximizing signal per token rather than adding more tokens; it's almost always better to repeat high-quality data than to show low-quality data. Data quality remains the single most underleveraged compute multiplier. He emphasizes this is a frontier research and engineering problem requiring the ability to score and understand data across many axes at petabyte scale. He closes by noting DatologyAI is hiring across multiple roles and invites interested parties to connect.
