## TL;DR
Mahesh Sathiamoorthy, co-founder and CEO of Bespoke Labs, presents the company's open-source work on curating high-quality data and reinforcement learning environments for post-training LLMs. He covers the creation of the Open Thoughts reasoning dataset, lessons learned in data curation (including several counterintuitive findings), a concrete enterprise post-training success story with Intuit's Credit Karma, and the emerging infrastructure stack needed to build and manage RL environments for autonomous agents.

## Key Takeaways
- Data and RL environments are the primary bottlenecks in post-training LLMs, while compute, models, and infrastructure are increasingly well-defined and commoditized.
- Bespoke Labs bridges the gap between data/RL environment creators and researchers by doing both, ensuring data curation is driven by what actually moves model metrics.
- The AI field has shifted from evaluating what models *know* (knowledge benchmarks) to what agents *do* (agentic benchmarks), with long-horizon autonomy gated by reliability.
- Post-training (SFT and RL) is a powerful lever for improving agent reliability and autonomy over long durations.
- Open Thoughts is a widely-used open-source reasoning dataset and curation recipe, developed with Stanford, UC Berkeley, and UW, demonstrating scalable improvements on benchmarks like AIME and LiveCodeBench.
- Several curation findings were counterintuitive: sampling multiple answers per question outperforms answering more unique questions; stronger models are not always better teachers; synthetic question generation worked better than answer filtering.
- For agentic training (Open Thoughts Agents), SFT still contributed the majority of gains, while RL provided the final few percentage points at high computational cost.
- A concrete enterprise deployment with Intuit's Credit Karma showed post-training can improve compliance, reduce latency, and lower costs by baking complex rules into a custom model rather than relying on lengthy prompts.
- Bespoke Labs built "Curator," a tool for curating reasoning data from prompts or logs, integrated with providers like Tinker and Fireworks.
- The emerging reference stack for post-training agents includes RL environment management, sandboxed rollout infrastructure with checkpointing/snapshots, and post-training methods (SFT, RL, and prompt optimization like JAPA).

## Detailed Breakdown

### Introduction and Bespoke Labs' Mission [00:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ewtOo0scUh0&t=12s)
Mahesh Sathiamoorthy introduces himself as co-founder and CEO of Bespoke Labs, previously a researcher and engineer at Google Deep Mind. Bespoke is an applied data research lab focused on helping enterprises and frontier labs access high-quality data and RL environments for post-training. He notes the importance of doing both data creation and consumption, as putting yourself in the researcher's shoes helps understand what moves model metrics.

### The Shift from Knowing to Doing and the Reliability Bottleneck [02:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ewtOo0scUh0&t=140s)
Mahesh traces AI's evolution from evaluating models on knowledge (STEM, humanities benchmarks) to evaluating agents on doing (SWE-bench, Terminal-bench). The ultimate goal is long-duration agent autonomy, but this is blocked by reliability—agents eventually fail by calling the wrong tool or making mistakes. Post-training is identified as a primary lever for improving reliability and extending autonomy, alongside prompting and harness updates.

### Data as the Bottleneck in Post-Training [04:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ewtOo0scUh0&t=297s)
While compute, models, and post-training infrastructure (providers like Fireworks, Tinker, Slime World) are well-defined, the major struggle for enterprises and frontier labs alike is access to high-quality data and RL environments. Mahesh frames RL environments as just "data in a very different shape," reinforcing that data remains the core bottleneck for both SFT and RL post-training.

### Open Thoughts: Curation of Reasoning Data [06:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ewtOo0scUh0&t=393s)
After DeepSeek's release revealed a lack of high-quality open reasoning data, Bespoke started "Bespoke Stratos," which evolved into the "Open Thoughts" consortium with Stanford, UC Berkeley, UW, and others. Published at NeurIPS, the work established a scaling law: as dataset size increases with their curation recipe, benchmark metrics (AIME, LiveCodeBench) improve. The dataset is widely used, cited by Microsoft's CSO and John Schulman at Thinking Machines.

### Open Thoughts Curation Pipeline and Learnings [08:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ewtOo0scUh0&t=519s)
The pipeline involves selecting source prompts, mixing questions (using LLMs to assess question quality/hardness), filtering, generating answers with teacher models (DeepSeek, Qwen, Gemini), and filtering answers. Key counterintuitive learnings: sampling multiple answers per question (e.g., 16 times) outperforms answering more unique questions once, likely due to reasoning diversity; stronger models are not always better teachers; synthetic question generation worked well while answer filtering did not.

### Open Thoughts Agents: Curating Data for Agents [11:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ewtOo0scUh0&t=708s)
The team extended their approach to agents with Open Thoughts Agents, establishing similar scaling laws for agentic data. Sources included Stack Exchange and others. Lessons echoed the reasoning work: stronger models (e.g., Claude) were not necessarily the best teachers (Qwen performed better), sampling multiple answers helped, and synthetic rewriting/task augmentation underperformed expectations. Notably, SFT contributed the majority of gains, with RL providing marginal improvements at high computational cost.

### Enterprise Case Study: Credit Karma (Intuit) [13:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ewtOo0scUh0&t=802s)
Mahesh shares a concrete production deployment with Intuit's Credit Karma app, which provides reasoning for credit card recommendations. Prompting frontier models failed compliance checks and blew up latency due to long rule lists. Post-training a custom model solved this, but data imbalance (e.g., overrepresentation of "0% APR") caused hallucination. The solution involved adding structural tags to prompt-response pairs, helping the model focus on form rather than specific numbers. This improved compliance, latency, throughput, and allowed Intuit to own the model and reduce costs as frontier models get more expensive.

### Curator Tool and the Emerging Post-Training Stack [15:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ewtOo0scUh0&t=953s)
Bespoke built "Curator," a tool for curating reasoning data from Hugging Face datasets or collected logs, integrated with Tinker and Fireworks. Mahesh then outlines the emerging reference stack for post-training agents: a layer for building and versioning RL environments and measuring quality; a lower infrastructure layer for sandboxes, rollouts, checkpointing, and snapshot/rollback for long-horizon tasks; and a top layer for post-training methods including SFT, RL, and JAPA (prompt optimization via LLM reflection). He notes convergence with similar ideas discussed by Ross earlier.
