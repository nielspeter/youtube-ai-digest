## TL;DR
Vasuman "Voss" Moza, CEO of Varick Agents, argues that AI has largely solved the execution of knowledge work, making the true bottleneck deep customer understanding and process re-engineering—the domain of the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE). He and his Head of Engineering, JD Puit, present Varick's internal "FD Agent," a custom-built tool designed to help their FDEs manage client context, build workflows, and eventually operate autonomously on routine client requests without exponentially scaling headcount.

## Key Takeaways
- AI execution is no longer the core bottleneck; the new constraint is understanding how unique businesses operate and re-engineering processes around AI.
- Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) map current human workflows, re-engineer them for AI, and deploy agents on top of existing enterprise systems.
- Slapping AI onto broken processes is why a vast majority of generative AI pilots fail to reach production or deliver measurable ROI.
- Enterprises are heavily married to their systems of record (NetSuite, SAP, Salesforce) and will not migrate, so AI solutions must be built on top of these existing platforms.
- Finding FDEs with both top-tier technical AI skills and the high EQ needed for client-facing work is extremely difficult.
- Varick Agents built an internal "FD Agent" to empower a single FDE to manage multiple complex client engagements simultaneously.
- The FD Agent has three stages: an Engagement Agent (assistant for synthesizing notes/docs), a Workflow Agent (embedded in the platform to catch edge cases during workflow building), and an Autonomous Agent (still in development) to handle minor client requests independently.
- Varick uses a dependency graph as a single source of truth for a company's functioning, as enterprise workflows are largely linear and dependency-driven.
- Frontier models like Claude are too verbose and lack the business intuition of human consultants, leading Varick to post-train open-source models (like Kimmy K26) for better analysis.
- Varick creates a reinforcement learning (RL) environment with custom tools to train models to reliably traverse complex knowledge graphs and extract the correct context.

## Detailed Breakdown

### The Next Bottleneck: Deep Customer Understanding [00:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=1s)
Voss opens by stating that while AI has solved the execution of end-to-end tasks, the remaining bottleneck is how deeply a company can understand a customer's unique business without exponentially scaling headcount. He notes that intelligence is no longer the constraint; harnesses and MCPs allow near-perfect execution. The challenge is extracting context from business teams and feeding it into models without them breaking down, as every business operates differently.

### The Role of the Forward Deployed Engineer [02:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=153s)
Operations are currently centered around humans, but Voss argues they will soon be centered around AI. The FDE's job is to go into a company, understand how things run today, and reimagine them for tomorrow. He outlines three core responsibilities of an FDE: mapping how humans currently do the work, re-engineering processes around AI, and deploying agents on top of existing systems.

### Mapping Human Workflows and Edge Cases [03:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=214s)
Voss explains that Varick embeds FDEs directly into specific departments (e.g., finance) to interview process leads. The goal is to understand not just the "golden path" documented in company materials, but the reality of what happens when things go wrong. He gives an example of an AP process where exceptions are handed off to a different person, adding days of cycle time—realities that are unique to every company and represent the true bottleneck for AI automation.

### Re-engineering Processes Around AI [05:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=307s)
Voss attributes the high failure rate of AI pilots (citing MIT statistics) to slapping AI onto broken processes. He argues that non-technical operators in finance or sales cannot get the same ROI from AI tools as software engineers do. FDEs must re-engineer workflows to be different enough to capture ROI (e.g., automating four of eight steps, keeping three with human-in-the-loop, and one fully manual) but not so different that adoption rates suffer.

### Deploying on Top of Systems of Record [07:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=430s)
Voss stresses that the AI wave left many enterprises behind because they are married to their systems of record like NetSuite, SAP, and Salesforce. He shares a client quote about spending $5 million and 5 years migrating to NetSuite, emphasizing that enterprises will not migrate off these systems for AI. Varick builds agents on top of these existing platforms via their "Varick OS" platform, providing governance and evaluation without requiring system migration.

### The Need for an AI FDE [08:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=524s)
While 2026 is being called the year of the FDE, Voss notes it is incredibly hard to find people who are both top 1% technically and possess the high EQ needed for client-facing work. To scale without becoming a traditional consultancy, Varick developed an AI FDE to bolster existing engineers, allowing one FDE to manage multiple high-maintenance client communications simultaneously.

### Introducing the FD Agent [12:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=741s)
JD Puit, Head of Platform, takes over to describe Varick's internal FD Agent, created after he noticed FDEs were sleep-deprived and struggling with verbose, incorrect outputs from uploading 150 pages of documentation to Claude. The FD Agent is designed to be the "Codeex for our FDEs" and has three stages of development.

### Engagement and Workflow Agents [13:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=802s)
The first stage is the Engagement Agent, an assistant that synthesizes Granola notes, documentation, and slides, allowing FDEs to query process ownership and resolve entity discrepancies (e.g., matching differently spelled names in emails and Slack). The second stage is the Workflow Agent, embedded inside the Varick platform, which sits alongside the FDE as they build workflows, proactively catching missed edge cases and ensuring the workflow correctly shadows the engineered process.

### The Vision for Autonomous Workflow Management [14:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=862s)
The third stage, still in development, is an autonomous assistant that can receive client emails (e.g., a request to change where a QC report is sent), query the company's knowledge graph, and ship autonomous changes to the workflow. This frees the FDE from minutia, allowing them to focus on high-value, in-person client interviews.

### Technical Architecture: Knowledge Graphs and Model Training [15:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=924s)
JD explains that Varick uses a dependency graph as a single source of truth for enterprise processes, noting that workflows are remarkably linear. He reveals that frontier models like Claude are too verbose and lack the consultant's intuition for what detail matters. To fix this, Varick post-trains open-source models (like Kimmy K26) to produce normalized process flows with the right balance of detail and clarity.

### RL Environments for Context Extraction [17:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=1047s)
The second technical challenge is reliably traversing the knowledge graph to extract the right context. Varick created a reinforcement learning environment with custom tools—such as one to verify if two differently spelled names refer to the same person, or one to identify redundancy cycles and DAG violations. They use this RL environment to train models to extract the correct context before generating analysis.

### Conclusion and Hiring [18:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0FLhNqBOic&t=1110s)
Voss wraps up by reiterating that Varick starts every engagement with a deep audit to learn how a business runs from the inside out. He emphasizes that while the fancy technology is necessary, the forward deployed motion is the true bottleneck and why Varick is bullish on their AI FDE. He invites interested companies and potential hires to find him after the talk, noting the company is aggressively hiring.
