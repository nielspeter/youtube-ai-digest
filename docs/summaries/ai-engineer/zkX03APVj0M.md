# Emulated: The Data for Fully Autonomous Software Engineers and Companies — Joseph Wang

## TL;DR
Joseph Wang and his co-founder Sid present Emulated, a data lab focused on improving the reliability and autonomy of AI agents by simulating entire software companies—including infrastructure, organizational context, and operational complexity—within containerized environments. They argue that current AI benchmarks are too narrow (limited to code diffs in single sandboxes), and that closing the model capability gap requires multi-node sandboxes with real cloud resources where agents must reason about distributed systems, live traffic, and long-horizon operational realities.

## Key Takeaways
- AI agents are proficient at the application layer but struggle with infrastructure reasoning (e.g., MVCC, distributed consensus, network partitions), and this gap is fundamentally a **data gap**.
- Current benchmarks like SWE-Bench Pro, Terminal Bench, and Frontier Code only test tasks within a codebase—producing a PR over 50–100 turns—missing the full scope of what human engineers actually do.
- Real software engineering involves PM work, customer conversations, performance testing, rolling deployments, monitoring, and owning infrastructure over years, not just producing a code diff.
- Emulated places entire software companies into containerized environments, including organizational context (projects, incidents, postmortems, customer conversations) and infrastructure complexity (network failures, data corruption, clock skew).
- A single-node sandbox can simulate a distributed cluster (flapping nodes, lagging learners, live traffic) but is insufficient for modeling AWS-scale services.
- Real infrastructure companies need resource provisioning, front-end APIs, throttling, authentication, deployment management, health monitoring, DNS/cert management, admin consoles, telemetry, and billing—all beyond what a single sandbox can represent.
- Emulated envisions **multi-node sandboxes with access to real cloud resources** ("a cloud in a box") as the future of training environments.
- Changing the sandbox paradigm so drastically also affects post-training pipeline infrastructure.
- The founders chose to start with infrastructure because it matches their domain expertise and because infra companies have clearer problem statements than companies still finding product-market fit.
- Open challenges include spinning up full stacks (e.g., AWS Lambda takes hours), cost management, and closing the sim-to-real gap even with real resources.

Joseph and Sid introduce themselves as co-founders of Emulated, a data lab focused on increasing AI agent reliability and autonomy. ### Background and the Model Capability Gap [01:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zkX03APVj0M&t=77s)
The founders describe their backgrounds in network infrastructure, distributed databases, and sandbox infrastructure—areas where workloads are mission critical. They reference the DynamoDB outage that took down US East 1 and half the internet, noting that models are proficient at the application layer but struggle with infrastructure reasoning, such as understanding MVCC on a database engine, which was a root cause of that failure.

### The Data Gap in Current Benchmarks [02:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zkX03APVj0M&t=160s)
Joseph explains that the model capability gap is really a data gap—models are only as good as their data. He surveys current benchmarks (SWE-Bench Pro, Terminal Bench, Frontier Code, Deep Sweep) and notes they only operate within the codebase: an agent gets a large task, produces a multi-thousand-line PR over 50–100 turns, but never performs the full range of human engineering work—PM tasks, customer conversations, performance testing, or long-term infrastructure ownership.

### Simulating Full Companies in Containerized Environments [03:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zkX03APVj0M&t=236s)
Sid describes Emulated's approach: putting entire software companies into containerized environments with organizational context (projects, incidents, customer conversations) and infrastructure-level issues (network failures, data corruption, clock skew). Agents must reason about orchestrating distributed clusters, operational blast radius, and live traffic—making tasks far more complex and long-horizon than a simple code diff.

### Example: An etcd Consensus Cluster [04:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zkX03APVj0M&t=290s)
Sid walks through a concrete example of an etcd consensus cluster that a production service might rely on. Old environments focused only on the etcd source code, but the real complexity lies in everything surrounding it: tickets, postmortems, customer sentiment, rolling deployments, hardware migration, failing nodes, stale deprecated nodes, and the requirement that the service never goes down while serving live traffic.

### Limits of Single-Node Sandboxes [06:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zkX03APVj0M&t=395s)
Joseph explains that Emulated simulates distributed clusters with multiple nodes, flapping nodes, and lagging learners within a single sandbox. While this gets you far, it is insufficient. Standard post-training pipelines are homogeneous—everything runs in Docker, everything is a single containerized sandbox—but real infrastructure and real companies do not work this way. Deterministic simulation of network failures doesn't capture what you encounter at AWS scale.

### What Real Infrastructure Companies Require [07:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zkX03APVj0M&t=460s)
Joseph walks through the full stack a real infrastructure company needs: a shiny piece of software, resource provisioning (EC2, Cloud Run, VPCs, subnets, security groups), a front-end API for customers, throttling, authentication, authorization, CloudTrail, deployment management with gradual rollouts and rollbacks, health monitoring with network partition awareness, configuration changes, DNS and cert management, admin consoles, telemetry, and billing. Beyond a certain threshold, single-node sandboxing cannot represent this complexity.

### Multi-Node Sandboxes with Real Infrastructure [10:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zkX03APVj0M&t=646s)
Sid states that beyond a critical mass, single-node sandboxing reaches its limit, and the future is environments that provision real infrastructure. Joseph describes this as a multi-node sandbox with access to real cloud resources—essentially "a cloud in a box." He notes this drastically changes post-training pipelines and mentions you can even put a post-training pipeline inside the sandbox, with interesting implications for model training and RSI.

### Why Share This and Open Challenges [11:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zkX03APVj0M&t=709s)
Joseph addresses why they are sharing their approach openly: they find these challenges fun and want to collaborate with others interested in working on them. He highlights unsolved problems: spinning up a full AWS Lambda stack takes hours (how does that fit a post-training rollout?), cost management, and the sim-to-real gap—real customer traffic and scale-dependent problems still exist even with real resources. He invites distributed systems engineers and people with model training experience to connect.

### Q&A: Primary Goal and Scope [13:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zkX03APVj0M&t=801s)
In Q&A, Joseph explains the name "Emulated": the real world is complex, and how the industry emulates it is contrived and low-fidelity. Their goal is to let agents own entire systems—and potentially entire companies—by emulating the real world at full fidelity. When asked whether they focus on infra/hardware or broader RL environments, he says they start with infrastructure because it matches their expertise and because infra companies have clearer problem statements (e.g., Modal users want low-latency, low-cost GPU sandboxes), whereas early-stage companies are still finding product-market fit. Sid adds that going deep on one vertical domain translates to other horizontal domains.
