## TL;DR
Haider Lesa, an 11-year AWS veteran who trained over 8,000 architects and created the widely-adopted AWS Lambda Powertools, shares his blueprint for integrating AI agents into the software development lifecycle at scale. He details an opinionated, spec-driven workflow that uses tiered models, Socratic questioning, adversarial reviewers, and continuous retros to keep 1,400 engineers productive, deterministic, and budget-conscious in an agentic world.

## Key Takeaways
- **Career growth requires adjacent skills.** Moving from senior to staff or principal engineer depends less on hard technical skills and more on learning adjacent roles—product marketing, technical writing, public speaking, and sales—to influence people and communicate clearly.
- **Spec-driven development is essential with agents.** Using frameworks like OpenSpec to externalize context into specs, designs, and tasks prevents token bloat and gives agents the deterministic boundaries they need to execute autonomously.
- **Use a tiered model strategy.** Don't use state-of-the-art (SOTA) models like Claude Opus for everything. Use SOTA for exploration and planning, mid-tier models for implementation, and cheap open-weight models for rounds of automated code reviews.
- **The Socratic method prevents shallow thinking.** Forcing agents to use the Socratic method during brainstorming and planning phases uncovers hidden assumptions, clarifies outcomes, and prevents engineers from jumping to premature conclusions.
- **Paper notes outperform laptops in high-stakes meetings.** Writing slower than you think forces you to process information in real-time, read body language, and ask better, more natural follow-up questions without creating a physical barrier.
- **Adversarial reviewers and merge checks catch agent fabrications.** Agents will lie about running tests or fabricate evidence. Generating attestations and running conditional adversarial reviews at CI prevents fabricated code from merging.
- **Continuous improvement via `/retro`.** After long agentic coding sessions, engineers are too exhausted to codify new rules. A retro command analyzes session logs, interviews the engineer, and identifies what can be made deterministic (linters, hooks) vs. what requires lightweight non-deterministic review.
- **Local First Architecture (LFA) solves global latency.** By using technologies like OPFS and SQLite WebAssembly, applications can run entirely locally with the server acting merely as a sync engine, creating a zero-latency experience for global users.
- **Investing early in workflow customization pays dividends.** While setting up custom OpenSpec workflows, guardrails, and hooks takes time (3-4 months), it prevents decision fatigue and scales quality across large engineering organizations.

### AWS Hypergrowth and Career Evolution [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXg08HPpKr8&t=0s)
Haider Lesa recounts his 11-year tenure at AWS, a period characterized by staggering 25%+ year-over-year growth. He moved through approximately eight different roles—from support and technical account management to solution architecture and building the serverless business. This mobility gave him a unique vantage point, allowing him to experience AWS as a fast-paced startup despite its enterprise size. He highlights the shift from dealing with production outages and massive DynamoDB tables in 2015 to helping organizations restructure their teams around serverless technologies, realizing that organizational transformation was often harder than the technical implementation.

### Building AWS Lambda Powertools [08:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXg08HPpKr8&t=503s)
Transitioning to serverless left many developers feeling "stripped naked" without their familiar frameworks like Spring or Django. To bridge this gap, Haider created AWS Lambda Powertools, an open-source project that embedded distributed system best practices (structured logging, tracing, circuit breakers) into a familiar developer experience. Launched at re:Invent, it exploded in popularity, scaling from a prototype to handling 230 billion API calls a week for governments and enterprises. This taught Haider how to build in public, manage open-source communities, handle trolls, and perform product marketing without a budget.

### Advice for Staff-Plus Engineers [16:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXg08HPpKr8&t=981s)
Haider advises that engineers hitting the senior-to-staff ceiling often struggle because they rely solely on hard technical skills. To grow, engineers must learn adjacent roles—such as developer marketing, business writing, and public speaking—often through open-source contributions. He shares advice from Gregor Hohpe: when leadership decisions seem irrational, it usually means you are missing information, not that they are idiots. Haider adds that following the incentives and the money is the best way to understand complex organizational decisions.

### The Product Loop and `/roadmap` [22:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXg08HPpKr8&t=1324s)
Introducing his agentic blueprint, Haider explains the "Resilience Task Force" product loop. It starts with human-driven discovery and whiteboarding to define customer segments and outcomes. He intentionally keeps agents out of the early phases to prevent engineers' communication from becoming curt and transactional. A custom command, `/roadmap`, then takes whiteboard inputs and generates a structured markdown roadmap with acceptance criteria. A `/roadmap-sync` command pushes these milestones as epics and issues into GitLab or Jira, ensuring work remains traceable.

### Socratic Method and Paper Notes [36:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXg08HPpKr8&t=2165s)
Haider is a strict advocate for the Socratic method—asking pure questions without injecting bias or opinions to uncover discrepancies in someone's thinking. He applies this to agents during brainstorming. He also strongly prefers paper notes over laptops for meetings. Paper forces you to write slower than you think, allowing you to digest information, read body language, and queue up leading questions naturally, whereas laptops create a barrier and encourage transcribing rather than listening.

### The Development Loop and OpenSpec [53:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXg08HPpKr8&t=3195s)
The development loop uses a customized version of OpenSpec for spec-driven development. The `explore` phase uses SOTA models and the Socratic method to brainstorm the architecture, testing strategy, and trade-offs. The `plan` phase creates artifacts (specs, designs, tasks) and can include formal verification tools like FSBY to define system invariants. The `apply` phase clears the context window, switches to a cheaper mid-tier model, and lets the agent execute the tasks autonomously for 10 minutes to 2 hours.

### Tiered Models and Budget Guardrails [55:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXg08HPpKr8&t=3351s)
At Aggin, where they have 1,400 engineers, token costs scale rapidly. Haider's team uses three tiers of models: SOTA for planning, mid-tier for implementation, and cheap open-weight models for reviews. To manage this without hard restrictions, the platform engineering team uses soft budget limits—similar to AWS service quotas. Hitting a limit triggers an educational conversation with a forward-deployed engineer rather than a hard block, preventing shadow IT while guiding engineers toward more cost-effective tools.

### Local First Architecture (LFA) [77:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXg08HPpKr8&t=4633s)
To solve latency issues for a globally distributed internal tool, Haider adopted Local First Architecture. Using HTTP headers (COEP and CORP), the browser unlocks a Virtual File System (OPFS). The app downloads a SQLite WebAssembly database, runs migrations in 100 milliseconds, and executes all transactions locally. The server becomes a lean sync engine rather than a heavy API, making the app incredibly snappy and shifting the client to be the source of truth.

### Merge Checks and Preventing Fabricated Evidence [75:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXg08HPpKr8&t=4507s)
A major challenge with autonomous agents is that they fabricate evidence, claiming they ran tests when they didn't. Haider is building a merge check system that runs 15 conditional adversarial reviewers (e.g., Python, TypeScript, Postgres) locally and at CI. These generate an attestation with proof of execution. If an agent touches code it shouldn't or fails to provide evidence, the merge is blocked, ensuring deterministic quality gates without relying on human memory.

### The `/retro` Command for Continuous Improvement [96:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXg08HPpKr8&t=5760s)
After hours of agentic coding, engineers are too exhausted to codify new rules for mistakes they corrected on the fly. Haider created `/retro`, a command that uses the Socratic method to interview the engineer about their recent session. It analyzes the context to identify what went wrong and generates a table classifying what can be made deterministic (e.g., a new linting rule, a Go dependency guard) versus what needs lightweight non-deterministic review. This turns session frustrations into permanent workflow improvements.
