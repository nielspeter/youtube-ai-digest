# GPT-5.6 just made itself CHEAPER

## TL;DR
OpenAI slashed the price of GPT-5.6 Luna by 80% and GPT-5.6 Terra by 20%, achieved by using their most powerful model, GPT-5.6 Soul, to recursively find efficiency gains in its own architecture and serving infrastructure. This represents a form of recursive self-improvement that gives frontier labs a massive competitive moat, making their smaller models extraordinarily cost-effective while they likely keep the efficiency gains from their largest models to increase margins.

## Key Takeaways
- OpenAI dropped the price of GPT-5.6 Luna by 80% (now $0.20 per million input tokens, $1.20 per million output tokens), making it cheaper than frontier open-source models from China.
- GPT-5.6 Terra received a 20% price drop, now costing $2 per million input tokens and $12 per million output tokens.
- GPT-5.6 Soul did not get a direct price drop, but its API fast mode was upgraded from 1.5x speed to 2.5x speed for the same 2x cost increase, effectively a price decrease.
- The massive price reductions were achieved by using GPT-5.6 Soul to analyze production traffic, optimize GPU kernels, improve speculative decoding, and rewrite production kernels—essentially the model improving its own efficiency.
- Cost per task completed is a more important metric than price per token; for example, Kimmy K3 is half the price of GPT-5.6 Soul but takes twice as many tokens, making them effectively the same price.
- On the Artificial Analysis intelligence index, GPT-5.6 Luna Max costs about 6 cents per task, vastly outperforming competitors like GLM 5.2 Max (26 cents), Grok 4.5 (50 cents), and Claude Sonnet 5 Max ($1.80) on a cost-to-intelligence basis.
- OpenAI likely kept the price of Soul the same despite finding efficiency gains because it is their primary revenue generator (cash cow), allowing them to increase margins instead.
- The competitive pressure from near-frontier open-source models like Kimmy K3 likely forced OpenAI to prioritize efficiency and aggressively cut prices.
- This self-optimization loop mirrors Andrej Karpathy's auto-research project, where an AI runs experiments to improve LLM training efficiency—except OpenAI is doing it at massive scale with a frontier model.
- The speaker speculates that OpenAI and Anthropic's playbook is to train massive, inefficient frontier models, use them to bake cheaper, efficient versions for the public, and keep the frontier models private to recursively train the next generation.

## Detailed Breakdown

### The 80% Price Drop for GPT-5.6 Luna [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAPDmc8e22U&t=0s)
OpenAI announced an 80% price cut for GPT-5.6 Luna, the smallest model in the GPT-5.6 family. The new pricing is $0.20 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens. The host emphasizes that this makes the model cheaper than frontier open-source models coming out of China, though he notes that price per token is less important than the total cost per task completed.

### GPT-5.6 Terra Price Cut and Soul Fast Mode [01:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAPDmc8e22U&t=93s)
GPT-5.6 Terra, the middle model, received a 20% price reduction, bringing it to $2 per million input tokens and $12 per million output tokens. The largest model, GPT-5.6 Soul, did not get a standard price drop. However, its API fast mode was upgraded: previously 1.5x faster for 2x the cost, it is now 2.5x faster for the same 2x cost increase, which acts as an effective price decrease for speed-intensive use cases.

### Cost Per Task vs. Price Per Token [02:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAPDmc8e22U&t=155s)
Using the Artificial Analysis intelligence index chart, the host demonstrates that GPT-5.6 Luna Max offers the best value. The chart plots overall intelligence against cost per task. Luna Max scores slightly higher in intelligence than GLM 5.2 Max but costs a fraction of the price (about 6 cents per task compared to 25-28 cents). Claude Opus 5 low is also comparable in intelligence but costs around 40 cents per task.

### Recursive Self-Improvement via GPT-5.6 Soul [04:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAPDmc8e22U&t=250s)
The most significant aspect of the announcement is that OpenAI used GPT-5.6 Soul, their frontier model, to make its own infrastructure more efficient. Soul analyzed production traffic, identified imbalances, tested routing strategies, and optimized production GPU kernels. This resulted in a 20% lower serving cost and 15% better token generation efficiency through improved speculative decoding. The host compares this to a continuous loop, where the model constantly monitors and optimizes its own performance 24/7.

### Soul Optimizing Its Own Architecture [07:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAPDmc8e22U&t=441s)
OpenAI's blog post reveals that Soul optimized the model's forward pass, found work that could be precomputed or parallelized, and rewrote production kernels. The model even improved its own draft model by designing and running hundreds of experiments on its architecture. The host notes this is stunning and expresses nervousness, as this level of recursive self-improvement gives top labs like OpenAI and Anthropic an almost insurmountable lead.

### Comparison to Karpathy's Auto Research Project [07:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAPDmc8e22U&t=473s)
The host draws a parallel between OpenAI's approach and Andrej Karpathy's open-source auto-research project. Karpathy's project used a loop to improve the efficiency of training a small language model from scratch, finding real improvements that Karpathy himself could not. The host imagines the scale of OpenAI running a similar loop with frontier compute and one of the best models on the planet, functioning as a fully automated AI researcher.

### Why Soul Didn't Get a Price Drop [09:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAPDmc8e22U&t=566s)
The host speculates on why GPT-5.6 Soul did not receive a price reduction despite likely achieving efficiency gains. He theorizes that Soul is OpenAI's cash cow, generating the majority of their revenue. While Luna and Terra may process more total tokens, the highest revenue comes from the most capable model. Therefore, OpenAI likely kept the efficiency gains from Soul to increase their profit margins rather than passing the savings to consumers.

### The Threat of Open Source and Kimmy K3 [10:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAPDmc8e22U&t=628s)
The host suggests that the release of Kimmy K3, a highly capable and seemingly inexpensive Chinese open-source model, likely pushed OpenAI to focus on efficiency and cut prices. While Kimmy K3's cost per task is effectively the same as GPT-5.6 Soul (half the price but twice the tokens), the perception of cheapness attracts users. Furthermore, as more researchers examine open-source models, the rate of efficiency discoveries may outpace what a closed team can achieve, prompting OpenAI to stay ahead.

### The Cost-to-Intelligence Landscape [11:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAPDmc8e22U&t=691s)
A chart comparing cost per intelligence index task shows GPT-5.6 Luna Max at $0.066, drastically cheaper than Claude Fable 5 ($2.75), GPT-5.6 Soul Max ($1.54), and Kimmy K3 ($0.72). The host highlights that Luna Max offers intelligence comparable to Claude Sonnet 5 Max but at 6 cents per task versus $1.80, placing it in a league of its own in the "top-left" green quadrant of the chart.

### Speculation on the Frontier Lab Playbook [12:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAPDmc8e22U&t=751s)
The host speculates that OpenAI and Anthropic share a common strategy: train massive, inefficient, and expensive frontier models, then use them to create smaller, highly efficient, and cheaper models for public consumption. He believes Anthropic may already be withholding their absolute frontier models (like Fable) from public release for extended periods. The revenue from the cheaper models funds the next generation of frontier models, which in turn train their successors—a cycle of recursive self-improvement that may make these labs impossible to catch.

### Open Source as the Only Competitive Pressure [14:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAPDmc8e22U&t=845s)
Because frontier labs have such a massive lead, momentum, and compute advantage, the host argues that open-source AI is the only thing applying competitive pressure on them. He concludes by noting that OpenAI's substantial price drop might change the math for consumers deciding between Anthropic and OpenAI subscriptions.
