# I Tested Opus 5 vs. Fable 5. What You Need to Know.

[![I Tested Opus 5 vs. Fable 5. What You Need to Know.](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/2J3uX8iRNng/hqdefault.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng)

## TL;DR
Nate Herk ran a series of head-to-head experiments comparing Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 and Fable 5 models within Claude Code on tasks ranging from bug hunting and diagram generation to building interactive simulators. While Opus 5 is half the per-token cost of Fable 5, it often used significantly more tokens and took longer due to aggressive verification loops, making it surprisingly more expensive in several tests. Fable 5 generally won on creative and visual design tasks, whereas Opus 5 excelled at following instructions and verifying its own work, though both models proved to be highly nondeterministic.

## Key Takeaways
- Opus 5 is half the per-token cost of Fable 5, but because it uses far more tokens for verification, it frequently ended up being more expensive per task.
- Fable 5 consistently outperformed Opus 5 on visual design, creative formatting, and tasks requiring a polished aesthetic (e.g., Excalidraw slides, LinkedIn carousels).
- Opus 5 is notably stronger at verifying its own work and iterating until it succeeds, aligning with Anthropic's release notes for the model.
- Both models are highly nondeterministic: running the exact same prompt through Opus 5 twice yielded drastically different results, costs, and execution times.
- For coding and bug-fixing tasks, results were mixed; in one test Fable produced a cleaner patch, but in another, Opus delivered a more accurate, benchmark-passing implementation.
- Opus 5 generated roughly 2 million output tokens across the experiments compared to Fable 5's 832,000, highlighting its token-heavy approach.
- Fable 5 was significantly faster on average, completing sessions in about 25 minutes on average compared to Opus 5's average of 60 minutes.
- For routine knowledge work like writing LinkedIn posts, both Opus 5 and Fable 5 are overkill; a lighter model like Sonnet 4.5 is sufficient.
- A practical workflow tip is to use Fable 5 as a high-level orchestrator that delegates tasks to Opus 5, conserving Fable's context window.
- Matching the intelligence of the model to the intelligence required for the task is critical for cost and time efficiency.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Introduction and Benchmark Surprise [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=0s)
The video opens with Nate noting that while Fable 5 remains Anthropic's most powerful model, benchmarks for the newly released Opus 5 show it outperforming Fable on metrics like the Frontier Bench, Cursor Bench, and coding agent index—at half the cost. Intrigued by these claims, Nate decides to test both models through his own real-world workflows within Claude Code to compare cost, time, and token efficiency.

### Excalidraw Diagram Generation Test [01:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=61s)
Nate begins with a subjective test in Claude Chat, asking both models to generate an Excalidraw diagram explaining semantic search with vectorization for AI agents. Fable 5's diagram was more visual but contained misspellings (e.g., "Large" and "Vectorize" not fully expanded) due to a lack of verification. Opus 5's layout felt more organized, detailed, and better suited for teaching, though less visually striking. Nate emphasizes that verification is crucial for AI agents, and this early test hints at Opus's strength in that area.

### Bug Hunting in a Large Codebase [03:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=185s)
In the first formal experiment, Nate tasks both models with exploring a large codebase to find and fix a bug, using Codex to review the outputs. In the first test, Fable 5 took 11 minutes and cost $5.30, while Opus 5 took 13 minutes and cost $4.22. Codex ranked them similarly, but Fable won due to a cleaner, immediately reviewable patch. In the second test with a different codebase, Opus 5 took 20 minutes and cost $6.50, while Fable 5 took 12 minutes and cost $8.73. Here, Opus 5 performed significantly better, passing 4 out of 4 tests (scoring 93/95) versus Fable's 2 out of 4 (scoring 66/95), delivering a more accurate, benchmark-passing implementation.

### Verification and Model Instruction [05:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=308s)
Nate highlights a key improvement in Opus 5 noted in Anthropic's release blog: it is much stronger at verifying its own work and iterating carefully until it succeeds. He explains that this means Opus can design tests to check if it has met the stopping condition, and keep iterating until the tests pass. However, he cautions that while the model matters, how you instruct the model and feed it context matters even more.

### Viral Video Generation Experiment [06:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=402s)
Nate asks both models to build a 10-second hyper-edited viral announcement video for an "AIS Live" event, allowing them to search his entire computer for context. Opus 5 created both vertical and landscape versions, taking 40 minutes and costing $11.19, though the result looked a bit "computery." Fable 5 created one video in 7 minutes and 49 seconds for about $7, but included outdated information about the event (e.g., speakers who didn't end up speaking). Nate notes this was partly his fault for not keeping his OS context updated, but suggests better verification could have caught the error.

### Landing Page Design Experiment [09:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=562s)
Both models were tasked with creating a one-page landing page for a certified AI consultant program. Opus 5 produced a wordy but accurate page with dynamic 3D elements and proper brand guidelines, taking nearly an hour and costing $35.83. Fable 5 created a very similar page with a nice card animation, taking 22 minutes and costing $20.50. Nate concludes that Fable 5 still has an edge on creativity and design, while being faster and cheaper in this instance, whereas Opus 5 excels at following directions and verification.

### LinkedIn Post and Carousel Experiment [12:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=748s)
Nate asks both models to write a LinkedIn post with a carousel, raising the stakes by saying it would be bad if it looked clearly AI-generated. Both models independently chose the same topic: AI agents went mainstream, but trust didn't. Nate preferred Fable 5's carousel, which used a tweet-style design he liked much more than Opus 5's "meh" styling. Fable 5 cost $6.17 and took 7.5 minutes, while Opus 5 cost $8.22 and took longer. Nate notes that even Opus 5 is overkill for this task—Sonnet 4.5 would suffice.

### Audience Pain Point Analysis [14:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=872s)
Both models were sent to Nate's YouTube channel and School community to analyze comments and threads, identify pain points, and suggest a product to build and a YouTube video to make. Both models produced very similar results, suggesting products like an "offer engine" or "AI consultant kit" and video titles about working with real businesses. Fable 5 cost $10.60 and took ~11 minutes, while Opus 5 cost $8.34 and took 20 minutes. Nate notes the results were so similar that trust would depend on which model looked through more data.

### YouTube Video Outline and Slide Deck [17:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=1030s)
Nate tasks both models with creating a YouTube video outline and an Excalidraw-style presentation, acting as a product manager that delegates and reviews. Both models independently chose the topic "context engineering for AI agents." Opus 5's slides didn't feel properly branded, while Fable 5's slides looked much more like Nate's typical style and were visually superior. Fable 5 took 8 minutes and cost ~$41, while Opus 5 took 1 hour and 15 minutes and cost $33. Nate awards the win to Fable 5 for better visuals and faster execution, despite the higher cost.

### Computer Use: Playing Snake [20:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=1218s)
Nate tests computer use capabilities by asking the models to use Play CLI to go to Google, play the snake game five times, screenshot the scores, and report the average. Due to a labeling error, Nate accidentally ran Opus 5 twice. The two identical Opus 5 runs produced drastically different results: one ran for 1 hour 53 minutes, cost $18, and went rogue playing 30+ games in batches; the other took 1 hour 10 minutes, cost $10, and followed instructions properly. This serves as a stark reminder of the nondeterministic nature of these models.

### Structural Stress Test Simulator [22:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=1372s)
In the final experiment, Nate asks both models to build a simulator for testing buildings and vehicles against weather, weights, and custom structures. Fable 5 produced a complex, overwhelming UI in just 7 minutes, costing $73. Opus 5 produced a simpler, more user-friendly UI in 2 hours and 26 minutes, costing $112. Nate was surprised that Opus 5's version was more approachable, attributing Opus's higher cost and longer runtime to its aggressive verification loops and stress testing. He notes Fable 5 could have done better but simply "decided to be done," underscoring how differently the models interpret instructions.

### Consolidated Results and Final Thoughts [28:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J3uX8iRNng&t=1681s)
Nate reviews the consolidated data: Opus 5 generated 2 million output tokens versus Fable 5's 832,000, with Opus averaging 60 minutes per session versus Fable's 25 minutes. Despite Opus 5's lower per-token cost, its token-heavy approach made it more expensive overall. Nate recommends using Fable 5 as an orchestrator that delegates to Opus 5 to conserve Fable's context window. He stresses that both Opus 5 and Fable 5 are often overkill for daily knowledge work, and users should match model intelligence to task requirements.
